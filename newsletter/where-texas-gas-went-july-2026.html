Where Texas gas went in July 2026: 0.81% flared, up from 0.73% a year before

Half went to processing plants and almost half to pipelines. The small share that was flared usually earns the owner nothing.

Royalty Clerk · Oct 31, 2026

Every month, each Texas operator reports where its lease's gas went. In July 2026, Texas leases reported moving 1.17 billion mcf of gas:

  • 49.9% to a processing plant
  • 46.6% to a transmission line
  • 2.3% burned as lease fuel
  • 0.81% (9.5 million mcf) vented or flared
  • the last 0.4% to other uses, such as gas lift and repressuring

The flared share was 0.73% in July 2025. This year it jumped to 1.26% in April and has come down since.

Where flaring changed most

Counties with the biggest change in flared gas, July 2026 against July 2025 (mcf flared, then the share of the county's gas):

  • Upton County: 902,044 mcf, 2.10% of its gas, up from 0.87%
  • Reagan County: 656,649 mcf, 1.27% of its gas, up from 0.73%
  • Winkler County: 416,560 mcf, 6.30% of its gas, up from 1.90%
  • Howard County: 979,496 mcf, 2.88% of its gas, up from 1.96%
  • Martin County: 588,511 mcf, 0.84% of its gas, down from 1.22%
  • Yoakum County: 445,644 mcf, 7.89% of its gas, up from 4.59%
  • Pecos County: 204,287 mcf, 2.35% of its gas, up from 0.57%
  • Ward County: 409,462 mcf, 2.13% of its gas, down from 3.10%

Reeves County flared the most gas of any county, 1.02 million mcf, but that is only 1.01% of its gas, up from 0.84%.

Why it matters for a royalty

Most Texas leases pay royalty on gas that is sold. Gas that is flared or burned on the lease is not sold, so it usually earns the owner nothing. If your lease's flared share jumps from one month to the next, it is fair to ask the operator why: a pipeline outage, a full plant, or a well not yet connected.

A switch between the plant code and the pipeline code can also change your statement, bringing plant products and processing fees onto it.
