33 years of Texas oil and gas records, 1993 to 2026

31.6 billion barrels of oil, 267 billion mcf of gas, and why a family looking for an old lease should start here.

Royalty Clerk · Oct 31, 2026

Our Texas production records now reach back to January 1993. They hold every monthly production report in the Railroad Commission's public data, more than 64 million filings across more than 350,000 leases, through August 2026.

Over those 33 years, Texas leases produced 31.6 billion barrels of oil and condensate and 267.4 billion mcf of gas.

The top oil counties since 1993

Barrels of oil and condensate, January 1993 through August 2026:

  • Midland County: 2.35 billion barrels
  • Martin County: 2.01 billion barrels
  • Reeves County: 1.79 billion barrels
  • Karnes County: 1.53 billion barrels
  • Loving County: 1.46 billion barrels
  • Upton County: 1.29 billion barrels
  • Andrews County: 1.18 billion barrels
  • Howard County: 1.16 billion barrels
  • Yoakum County: 959 million barrels
  • De Witt County: 865 million barrels

The shale years show up plainly. In Midland, Martin, Reeves, Loving and Howard counties, more oil came out between January 2020 and August 2026 than in the 27 years before. Midland produced 872 million barrels from 1993 through 2019, and 1.48 billion since. Karnes runs the other way: its Eagle Ford boom came in the 2010s, with 852 million barrels before 2020 and 678 million since.

Tarrant is the Barnett Shale under Fort Worth. Webb and Zapata sit on the border near Laredo.

Why old records matter to a family

Many people find us after a parent or grandparent dies and a check stub or a tax bill turns up with a lease name on it. With records back to 1993, a lease page shows that lease's whole life: when it started producing, which operators ran it, and whether it still produces today. A lease that still produces is usually still in force, which means someone is still owed royalty on it.
