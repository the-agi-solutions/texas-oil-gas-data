What 6.8 million Texas mineral accounts can tell an heir

We now search more than 140 county appraisal rolls. Here is what a roll is, which rolls are largest, and how to look for a family name.

Royalty Clerk · Oct 31, 2026

Texas counties tax producing mineral interests like any other property. Each county's appraisal district keeps a roll with an account for every interest it taxes, in the owner's name, down to very small fractions of a lease.

We now search more than 140 of those county rolls, holding 6.8 million mineral accounts. About 81% of the accounts match a Railroad Commission lease, which is how we connect a name on a roll to the oil and gas behind it.

Tarrant's roll is the largest by far because the Barnett Shale runs under Fort Worth and its suburbs, where many homeowners hold a small mineral interest.

Why a roll helps when someone dies

An heir often does not know what a parent owned. The appraisal roll is one of the few public places where a mineral interest appears under a person's name. If a name is on a roll, the county believes that person owns a producing interest, and a tax bill has been going out for it, often to an old address.
