Operator changes, July 2026: 1,850 Texas leases got a new operator

One sale moved 1,139 leases. Here is who took over what, and how to keep your checks from falling through the gap.

Royalty Clerk · Oct 31, 2026

In July 2026, 1,850 Texas leases were first reported under a new operator, in 69 moves between operators. Sixty companies gained leases.

The biggest moves

  • Diversified Production LLC to Eagleridge Operating, LLC: 1,139 leases
  • Jay Management Company, LLC to Great American Resources Inc.: 214 leases
  • 84 Energy LLC to Iron Jaw Operating LLC: 112 leases
  • Overton Park Oil & Gas LP to Hilcorp Energy Company: 48 leases
  • Roy Jackson Energy Operating to Lakeshore Operating, LLC: 41 leases
  • Eagle Oil & Gas Co. to Fortune Resources LLC: 38 leases
  • Walsh & Watts, Inc. to United Oil & Shale LLC: 27 leases
  • Durango Resources Corp. to Aransas Operating Company, LLC: 25 leases

July was not unusual. Between January and July 2026, 15,796 Texas leases changed operators, with the biggest months in January (4,291) and May (6,167). August shows 589 so far, but its reports are still coming in.

Why a new operator matters to an owner

The operator is usually the company that pays your royalty. When a lease is sold, the old company stops paying and the new one has to set you up from scratch: your name, address, decimal interest and tax ID. The months on either side of the switch are when a payment is most often missed.

What to watch for:

  • A letter or a new division order from the new company. Check that the decimal matches your old check stubs before you sign. Under Texas law a division order does not amend your lease.
  • A gap of a month or two is common. If it runs longer, write to the new operator and include the owner number from your old stub.
  • Money held in suspense while the paperwork catches up still belongs to you, and Texas law adds interest to late royalty payments.
