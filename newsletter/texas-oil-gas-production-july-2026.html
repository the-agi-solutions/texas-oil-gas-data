Texas oil and gas, July 2026: oil down 11.6% on a year before, gas flat

What the Railroad Commission's newest production file shows, county by county, and what it means for the checks arriving now.

Royalty Clerk · Oct 31, 2026

The Texas Railroad Commission's newest production file came out in late October. It fills in July 2026 and brings the first reports for August. Here is what it shows, and what it means for a royalty check.

Texas as a whole

In July 2026, 138,490 Texas leases reported 159.5 million barrels of oil and condensate and 1.17 billion mcf of gas.

  • Oil is down 11.6% on July 2025, when it was 180.4 million barrels.
  • Gas is nearly flat, down 0.3% on July 2025.
  • Oil is up 0.9% on June 2026 (158.1 million barrels).

One caution: operators are still filing late reports for July, so its totals will rise a little and the gap with last year will narrow. July 2025 has had a year to settle. August has 124,633 leases reporting so far, too early to read.

The top ten oil counties

Barrels of oil and condensate in July 2026, against July 2025:

  • Martin County: 20.37 million barrels (down 6.2%)
  • Midland County: 19.61 million barrels (down 6.0%)
  • Reeves County: 13.63 million barrels (down 11.8%)
  • Loving County: 13.34 million barrels (down 13.7%)
  • Upton County: 9.12 million barrels (up 0.4%)
  • Reagan County: 6.93 million barrels (up 9.1%)
  • Karnes County: 5.86 million barrels (down 36.5%)
  • Howard County: 5.65 million barrels (down 35.2%)
  • Andrews County: 5.40 million barrels (down 11.6%)
  • Ward County: 4.84 million barrels (down 4.6%)

Karnes and Howard stand out, both down by more than a third. That can come from late reports, fewer new wells coming on, or older wells declining. It does not mean anyone's royalty is wrong, but if your lease is in one of those counties, look at your own lease's numbers.

What it means for your check

Texas law gives operators 60 days after the end of the month oil is sold to pay for it, and 90 days for gas. So the checks arriving now mostly pay for July and August production. A check is roughly volume times price, less taxes and any deductions your lease allows. If yours dropped, the first question is whether your lease produced less, or whether the price or the deductions changed.
